# INKLINE art direction

Use a thin black figure with a round head, narrow limbs, and short rounded hands and feet. Keep the head blank by default. Use strong readable poses. Check each pose from the front, side, and overhead views.

The style draws from Flash stick-figure animation. The references include Xiao Xiao, Animator vs. Animation, and Shock. Use their clear silhouettes and fast pose changes as direction. Do not copy their characters, scenes, frames, or logos.

Use warm paper (`#eeece5`) for the background. Use off-white (`#eeeae1`) for large object faces. Use charcoal (`#151716`) for figures and held equipment. Use lighter gray for environment structure. Keep large walkable faces light. A black deck must not hide a black foot. Use orange (`#d45538`) for small accents, signals, and selected effects.

All model materials are unlit. Avoid photo textures, gloss, bloom, and surface noise. Model useful structure into the silhouette. Use a small number of hard edges to explain depth. Keep construction details large enough to read on a phone.

Industrial parts use a common scale. Main architectural parts follow 2 m and 4 m grids. Some dressing parts have custom dimensions. Use the manifest and supplied scene to check scale and connection points.

Effects use crisp strokes, tapered arcs, impact stars, rings, sparks, chips, and outline dust. A burst must have a clear start and end. Larger impacts may combine a core, rays, and a ring. Avoid large transparent smoke planes in the runtime.

The generated [Study 03 reference](reference/study-03.png) guided the shape and palette. The final screenshots and previews show the actual 3D assets. Judge runtime quality from those images. The pack includes original geometry and effect output only.

## Art review rules

Read the figure before the environment. Keep floor seams thin and low in contrast. Use orange for small points of interest. Avoid large black building faces behind a black character. Keep shadow marks on the support surface when a character jumps.

Check preparation, contact, and recovery without effects. Open the elbow and knee shapes. Keep a clear gap between the guard arm and the striking arm. Use a short strike interval and a longer recovery. Check the forward reach in the exported GLB, not only in the source bone angles.

Place the camera to separate the player and the target. Use the expanded stage for close action review. Test the normal interface at 390 pixels as well. A close view must keep the feet and the landing surface visible.

Make hit effects small enough to show the contact pose. Use a paper core inside a dark impact star. Keep checkpoint effects at the feet. Do not use large rings across the body. Camera impulses stop when the viewer requests reduced motion.

The review video must show actions at normal speed. Use actual runtime captures for approval. Concept art sets direction; it does not prove asset quality.

## Character roles

Use six families: Balanced, Swift, Compact, Reach, Power, and Guard. Change real body landmarks and stance within the common stick figure style. Keep the head close to the shoulder branch. Preserve round stroke ends and open spaces between limbs. Equipment must explain a role through one clear shape. A shield, staff, or pistol is useful. Small unrelated bands are not a substitute for body variation.

Read [the source study](flash-reference-study.md). [Study 04](reference/study-04-characters.png) sets the character direction. Its [generation record](reference/study-04-prompt.txt) states the mode and direction. Compare that concept with the actual body and role sheets in the review page.


## Kinetic refinement

Use the contact pose as the strongest point in an action. Give the target enough travel to show the force. Keep the camera response smaller than the body response. Use the hand, foot, or mounted tool path for a short ribbon. Do not add a wide second limb beside the figure.

Use a short trace for a shot. Stop it at the nearest visible target or scene surface. A blocked shot must not damage a target behind the surface. Use the chest bone to place the target contact point on each body size.

Keep dust open and uneven. Use a few growing strokes with a fade. Avoid a row of closed rings at the feet. Keep crisp paper cores for direct impacts. Do not use full-screen flashes or color inversion.

Check real playback beside the saved previous pass. Inspect the same action at normal speed from a side view and a perspective view. Also check the phone layout, all four game cameras, and Reduced motion.
